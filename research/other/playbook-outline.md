# Playbook Outline

Working title:

**The B2B SaaS AI SEO Engine: From Expert Inputs to AI-Visible Pipeline**

## Who This Playbook Is For

- B2B SaaS founders who need organic demand but do not have a large content team.
- Heads of marketing who want to use AI without damaging brand trust.
- Content and SEO leads who need to update their workflow for AI search, AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity, and zero-click behavior.

## Positioning

Most AI SEO advice is still framed around producing more content. I think the better angle is producing **more defensible content**: pages with a real reason to exist.

The promise:

> Build an AI-assisted SEO system that turns product knowledge, customer language, and expert insight into content buyers trust and AI systems can cite.

## Chapter 1: Pick The Right Content Battles

Goal: Stop making pages just because a keyword exists.

Core ideas:

- Map buying-stage intent before keyword volume.
- Prioritize pages that can influence demos, trials, sales calls, or expansion.
- Separate content into create, refresh, merge, distribute, and skip.
- Use AI to find patterns, not to decide strategy alone.

Recommended assets:

- Alternatives pages
- Comparison pages
- Use-case pages
- Integration pages
- Problem/solution pages
- Customer proof pages
- "How we do X" operational pages

## Chapter 2: Build A Differentiated Input Library

Goal: Give AI better raw material than competitors have. This is probably the biggest difference between good AI content and bland AI content.

Inputs to collect:

- Customer call transcripts
- Sales objections
- Support tickets
- Product demos
- Founder opinions
- SME interviews
- Internal benchmarks
- Case studies
- Customer quotes
- Review-site language
- Competitor comparison notes

AI role:

- Cluster repeated pain points
- Pull buyer language
- Extract objections
- Suggest content angles
- Turn transcripts into outlines
- Find missing proof points

Human role:

- Choose the strategic angle
- Add judgment
- Validate claims
- Protect positioning
- Decide what not to publish

## Chapter 3: Create AI-Visible Content Architecture

Goal: Make pages useful for humans and easy for AI systems to retrieve.

Page structure principles:

- Start with a direct answer.
- Use descriptive headings.
- Include named entities and specific product context.
- Add examples, numbers, screenshots, demos, and proof.
- Break complex ideas into clear passages.
- Link related pages tightly.
- Include comparison tables where buyers naturally compare options.

Avoid:

- Long generic introductions
- Unverifiable claims
- Keyword stuffing
- Thin listicles
- AI-generated summaries without examples
- Pages that answer a broad topic but not a buyer's real decision

## Chapter 4: Use AI For Production, Not Autopilot

Goal: Scale the boring parts without outsourcing the thinking.

Draft workflow:

1. Research buyer intent and SERP/AI answer patterns.
2. Pull differentiated inputs from the input library.
3. Ask AI to cluster questions and objections.
4. Draft a brief with audience, buying stage, proof points, and desired action.
5. Generate an outline.
6. Add human point of view and examples.
7. Draft sections with AI assistance.
8. Edit for clarity, originality, and accuracy.
9. Add internal links and conversion paths.
10. Publish, distribute, and track.

Quality gates:

- Does this page say something a competitor could not easily say?
- Does it help a buyer make a decision?
- Does it include proof?
- Does it connect to a product or business outcome?
- Can a passage from this page answer a precise question?

## Chapter 5: Distribution As SEO

Goal: Create signals outside the company blog. This matters because the company blog is not the only place buyers, or AI systems, learn who to trust.

Distribution channels:

- Founder and expert LinkedIn posts
- YouTube explainers
- Podcast appearances
- Partner articles
- Community discussions
- Reddit answers where appropriate
- Review pages
- Comparison pages
- Customer stories

Why it matters:

- Buyers trust third-party mentions.
- AI systems summarize information from across the web.
- Repetition across credible sources strengthens entity associations.
- Distribution creates demand even when clicks from search decline.

## Chapter 6: Measure Beyond Traffic

Goal: Connect AI SEO content to business outcomes, not just traffic charts.

Metrics to track:

- AI citations and mentions
- Branded search growth
- Demo/trial assists
- Conversion rate by content type
- Sales-call mentions
- Pipeline influenced
- Page refresh lift
- Internal-link contribution
- Rankings and clicks
- Share of voice in AI answers

Bad measurement:

- Only publishing volume
- Only traffic
- Only keyword rank
- Only AI visibility screenshots without business context

## Chapter 7: 30-Day Implementation Plan

Week 1:

- Audit existing content.
- Build a list of bottom-funnel opportunities.
- Collect 10 customer calls or sales notes.
- Pick 5 pages to refresh or create.

Week 2:

- Build briefs for the 5 pages.
- Interview SMEs.
- Draft outlines.
- Decide proof points and conversion paths.

Week 3:

- Produce the pages.
- Add internal links.
- Edit for originality and accuracy.
- Publish first refreshes.

Week 4:

- Distribute through LinkedIn, newsletter, sales team, partners, and communities.
- Track citations, rankings, conversions, and sales feedback.
- Decide the next 5 pages using the rubric.

## Final Recommendation

My strongest takeaway: B2B SaaS teams will not win by publishing the most AI content. They will win by building a better system for turning real expertise into content that buyers can use, AI systems can retrieve, and sales teams can actually benefit from.

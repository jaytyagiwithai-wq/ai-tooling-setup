# Content Decision Rubric

Purpose: give a simple way to decide whether a B2B SaaS team should create, refresh, merge, distribute, or skip an AI-assisted SEO asset.

Score each category from 0 to 3.

```text
0 = weak or missing
1 = somewhat present
2 = strong
3 = very strong / clear advantage
```

## Rubric

| Category | Question | Score |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buyer intent | Does this topic map to a real buying question, pain, objection, or use case? | 0-3 |
| Revenue proximity | Can this content influence demos, trials, sales conversations, pipeline, expansion, or retention? | 0-3 |
| Differentiated inputs | Do we have customer language, SME insight, product data, examples, or original proof competitors cannot easily copy? | 0-3 |
| Product relevance | Can the content naturally connect to what the product does without forcing it? | 0-3 |
| AI search fit | Could this page answer a precise question that AI systems may summarize or cite? | 0-3 |
| Search demand | Is there evidence of search demand, AI-answer demand, community demand, or sales-team demand? | 0-3 |
| Competitive gap | Can we produce something meaningfully better, clearer, newer, or more useful than what exists? | 0-3 |
| Distribution potential | Can this be repurposed into LinkedIn posts, YouTube, newsletter, sales enablement, partner content, or community discussion? | 0-3 |
| Measurement clarity | Do we know what success looks like beyond traffic? | 0-3 |
| Maintenance cost | Can we keep this accurate as product, market, or AI search behavior changes? | 0-3 |

Maximum score: 30. The score is not meant to be perfect; it is meant to slow down bad content ideas before they become 2,000-word AI drafts.

## Decision Rules

| Score | Decision | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 24-30 | Create or heavily refresh | Prioritize now. Use SME input and make it a flagship asset. |
| 18-23 | Create after strengthening inputs | Good opportunity, but collect more proof, examples, or a sharper distribution angle first. |
| 12-17 | Refresh, merge, or narrow scope | The topic may be useful, but it is probably too generic or too weak as a standalone page. |
| 0-11 | Skip | Do not create with AI just to fill a calendar. |

## Action Types

### Create

Create when:

- Buyer intent is strong.
- Product relevance is obvious.
- The team has differentiated inputs.
- The page can be distributed and measured.

### Refresh

Refresh when:

- The page already ranks or converts but is outdated.
- AI search behavior has changed the answer format.
- Competitors have better examples, structure, or proof.
- Internal product positioning has changed.

### Merge

Merge when:

- Multiple thin pages answer the same buyer question.
- The content competes with itself.
- The search intent is better served by one authoritative page.

### Distribute

Distribute when:

- The page is good but invisible.
- The content needs third-party validation.
- The topic would work as LinkedIn posts, YouTube clips, partner content, or sales enablement.

### Skip

Skip when:

- The topic is only keyword-driven.
- The team has no original insight.
- The page cannot connect to the product or buyer journey.
- Success would be measured only by vanity traffic.

## Example Scoring

Hypothetical topic:

**"Best AI SEO tools for B2B SaaS teams"**

| Category | Score | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buyer intent | 3 | Buyers actively compare tools. |
| Revenue proximity | 2 | Can influence vendor consideration if the product is relevant. |
| Differentiated inputs | 1 | Weak unless the team has testing data or strong POV. |
| Product relevance | 2 | Depends on the SaaS category. |
| AI search fit | 3 | AI systems often summarize tool comparisons. |
| Search demand | 2 | Likely demand, but competitive. |
| Competitive gap | 1 | Many generic tool lists already exist. |
| Distribution potential | 3 | Easy to repurpose into posts, videos, and sales material. |
| Measurement clarity | 2 | Can track demos, clicks, and mentions. |
| Maintenance cost | 1 | Tool lists require frequent updating. |

Total: 20

Decision:

Create only if the team can add original testing, strong criteria, product-specific use cases, and a maintenance plan. Otherwise, narrow it into a more defensible page such as:

**"How B2B SaaS teams should evaluate AI SEO tools for pipeline, not traffic."**
